Booking Journey: RWC2027 Pilot Plan

Working draft for the Rugby World Cup 2027 pilot, built on top of the existing Booking Journey overview. Structured around what we actually know about how these packages sell, not a single fixed itinerary.

📌 Status: working draft, not yet confirmed as the pilot event. Pending Nicky's parallel check and final sign-off. Enquiry-naming cleanup is a known separate issue (see Enquiry Naming Convention project) and is not a blocker for this build.

Why RWC2027, and why it's different

Open enquiries are heavily concentrated on RWC2027 right now (138 open enquiries, R70.98M), including many already tagged for Quarter Finals, Semi Finals, and Finals stages, not just the three confirmed pool matches. That tells us real demand assumes a deep Springbok run, not just the guaranteed pool stage.

Unlike a single-day Grand Prix, RWC2027 packages fall into three genuinely different products, because knockout-stage tickets are sold as a seat at a fixed match session (Quarter-Final 1, Semi-Final 2, etc.), not a guaranteed South Africa fixture. The customer is booking a shot at seeing the Boks play, not a confirmed matchup. That uncertainty is the emotional core this Journey needs to work with, not around.

The three booking tiers

Tier A · Pool Stage Only

Individual match packages or "Follow South Africa Pool Stages" · R22,885–R72,716 pp sharing · fixed opponent, fixed city, fixed dates

Adelaide (v Italy, 3 Oct), Brisbane (v Georgia, 10 Oct), or Perth (v Romania, 17 Oct). Highest certainty, so content can be specific and practical from day one: named opponent, named city, named stadium.

Tier B · Progression Packages

Round of 16, Quarter Finals to Semi Finals, Quarter Finals to Finals · R35,256–R122,906 pp sharing · opponent and sometimes venue unconfirmed

Customer has bought into the Boks going deep. Content has to work without knowing the opponent, and needs to hold interest through a run of pool results that will either validate or complicate the trip.

Tier C · Semis / Finals Focus

Semi Finals to Finals · from R115,176 pp sharing · Sydney, longest lead time (up to 15+ months for early bookers)

Highest spend, longest wait, least certainty. This is the tier "keep the dream alive" matters most for, since the trip's entire premise depends on results that won't be known for the better part of a year.

Core purpose

This Journey exists to sustain excitement across a long gap between deposit and departure, not to deliver logistics reminders. Logistics content (visas, packing, check-in) still belongs in the series, but placed later, closer to travel, so it doesn't crowd out the anticipation-building content that's the actual point of the series in the Medium/Long stretch.

Content ideas by type

All tiersCountdown milestones — 300/200/100/50 days to kickoff, timed to land as meaningful check-ins across a long gap rather than an arbitrary schedule.
All tiersSquad and form news — Rassie's squad announcements, warm-up results (Nations Championship, Rugby's Greatest Rivalry v New Zealand), building credibility for the "three-peat" narrative as the tournament approaches.
All tiersNostalgia — 2019 and 2023 wins, iconic moments, building emotional stake ahead of a third consecutive title bid.
Tier ACity spotlights — Adelaide, Brisbane, or Perth, matched to the customer's actual fixture. Local highlights, getting around, what to do on a free day.
Tier B / C"Path to the Final" explainer — plain-language walkthrough of what has to happen (pool position, round of 16 draw) for their ticket to become a Springbok match. Genuinely useful, since the tournament format is new (24 teams, round of 16 introduced for the first time) and likely unfamiliar even to regular fans.
Tier B / CPool result reactions — a short send after each SA pool match (3, 10, 17 Oct) confirming what it means for their trip. Best treated as a manually-triggered/curated send layered onto the Journey rather than a fixed automated step, since it depends on real results.
Tier CSydney spotlight — since this tier's destination is fixed regardless of who's playing, city content can be built early and confidently, unlike the opponent question.
Late-stage, all tiersPractical prep — visas, passport validity, insurance, e-SIM, packing. Same content shape as the existing Long/Medium/Short window mailers, reused here rather than rebuilt.

Backfill vs two parallel journeys

Build/send planned to start January 2027. Since many customers will already be booked well before then, two options:

OptionWhat it means
One-time backfillExisting bookers enter the same linear Journey mid-sequence, at the step matching their time-to-travel. Simpler build, but risks a jarring first send if it lands awkwardly against real-world timing.
Two parallel journeysOne for past-booked-but-still-to-travel customers (entry logic built around their existing travel date), one for customers booking from January onward (standard entry at the first step). More build work, but avoids the mid-sequence awkwardness, and Tier A/B/C already means multiple tracks exist anyway.
Given three tiers already exist by package type, and remaining wait time varies hugely within RWC2027 bookers (a Tier A pool-only booker in September has a very different runway to a Tier C Finals booker who booked in early 2026), the two-journeys approach looks like the better fit here, worth confirming once the tier/backfill build is scoped properly.

Open questions

Tier assignment mechanism — how do we reliably identify which tier a customer booked into? Package name on the Enquiry, a dedicated field, or something else. Needs a Fresh-side answer before content can be routed correctly.
Pool-result-triggered sends — do these get built as manual curated sends layered on top of the Journey, or is there a lighter automated trigger worth exploring once the core Journey is stable?
Tier movement — if the Boks progress, does a Tier A or B customer get any acknowledgement of that even though it doesn't change their own ticket, or does content stay strictly scoped to what they actually booked?
